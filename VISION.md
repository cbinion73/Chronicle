# Chronicle — The Vision

> *"…so that when your children ask, 'What do these stones mean?' you can tell
> them."* — Joshua 4:6–7

This is Chronicle's founding document. [REDESIGN.md](REDESIGN.md) records
*how* each milestone was built; [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) records *what* we
build next and in what order. This document records **why** — the identity,
the design language, and the full map of where this product is going. Every
future milestone should be able to point at a line in this file and say:
that's the stone I'm laying.

---

## What Chronicle Is

**Chronicle is the family Bible, reborn.**

For three hundred years the family Bible held births, deaths, and baptisms
handwritten in its front matter. Marginalia accumulated in two or three
different hands across generations. It fell open at the loved pages. It was
the family's record of God's faithfulness, kept *inside* the Scriptures
themselves, and passed down as the single most important object in the house.

That is the category Chronicle belongs to — a category of one. It is not a
journaling app and not a Bible app. It does not compete with either. It
succeeds an artifact that predates both by centuries.

The mission it serves is unchanged from day one: **Know, Understand, Live,
Teach, Pass On** the Scriptures. What this document adds is the form the
mission takes: *a line, stones along it, kept in liturgical time, with
ceremonies at the sacred moments, compiling continuously into a real book.*

## The Design Language

The product already contained its own design language before we named it.
It is in Joshua 4: memorial stones, set up so the next generation will ask
what they mean. The Baptist Beads already carry 68 photographed stones.
The nav already speaks in rooms and threads. The language was in the assets
folder all along; the work is committing to it everywhere.

### 1. The Thread made literal
One continuous line — your walk with God — running through the entire
product. Not a metaphor in the nav labels; a visible, drawn line. Today is
always the growing tip. Zoom out and the line runs back through months and
years to your first entry. The Record, the Answered Light, the Growth Spine,
and the Story are not sibling tabs; they are **altitudes** of the same line.
Zoom all the way in: today's entries. Zoom out: only the stones remain.

### 2. Stones, not cards
Sacred moments — a baptism, a calling, a prayer answered after four years —
must not render as the same rounded rectangle that shows a settings toggle.
They are set as stones along the line, with the weight and texture of the
Beads. The Growth Spine is a cairn, not a timeline of cards.

### 3. The app keeps liturgical time
A Daily Office that looks identical at 6am and 11pm is missing its own
point. The interface knows the hour: morning opens with the Call to
Worship, warm and bright; evening opens with examen — *here is what you
wrote today, here is what you carried* — in a darker, quieter register.
Sunday feels different from Tuesday. The church calendar becomes a design
system: Advent, Lent, and Eastertide each subtly re-tone the whole app.

### 4. Ceremony over CRUD
Five or six actions in this product are sacred, and they must not pass
through the same modal used to fix a typo. Marking a prayer answered is a
ceremony: the request visibly moves from the list into the light, with a
beat of stillness, and the answer is written in your own words as the
closing act. Setting a growth marker sets a stone. Deletes get undo, never
`window.confirm` — the interface never startles.

### 5. Grace at re-entry
The dry season is not an edge case; it is the design brief. Returning
after a gap must never be met with a counter of what was missed. The app
opens with what you recorded: *"Welcome back. Here is what you were
carrying when you were last here. Two of those prayers have been waiting
for you to see them answered."* Luke 15 as interface behavior — the father
saw him while he was still a long way off. A thread that goes quiet is
never deleted, never flagged, never shamed. The braid keeps a place.

### 6. The Book is real
"The Book of Chris" is not generated prose in a card. It is an actual
book: typeset pages, chapters broken at growth markers, years as parts,
printable as a bound-quality PDF. The count is visible: *"You are on page
214 of your book."* You are not journaling into a database — you are
writing a book for your grandchildren, one day at a time. That sentence is
why a person opens the app.

## The Map — Hour to Century

The vision covers every timescale a human life touches. Each ring contains
the previous.

### Ring 1 — The Place (the hour and the day)
Everything in the Design Language above: the line, the stones, the hours,
the ceremonies, the register of quiet. Plus **chapel mode**: one verse,
no chrome, nothing to tap. Silence as a first-class screen.

### Ring 2 — Time Becomes Bidirectional (the season and the year)
- **Sealed prayers.** Write a prayer and seal it — "open on my daughter's
  wedding day," "open in ten years." It sits visibly on the path ahead,
  seen but untouchable. Waiting as a designed experience; Advent logic.
- **Resurrection of your own words.** Reading Psalm 34 on a hard morning,
  Chronicle quietly notes: *you clung to this passage in March 2027; here
  is what you wrote then.* Echoes, extended from Scripture to your own
  life. Your past self ministering to your present self.
- **Personal feast days.** The church gave us Advent and Easter; your
  thread generates a second calendar. The anniversary of your baptism. The
  date the four-year prayer was answered. Chronicle keeps these feasts,
  every year, unprompted.
- **Patina.** A physical Bible falls open at the loved pages; the corners
  of Psalm 23 go soft from forty years of handling. Chronicle's Bible
  wears the same way — passages you have lived in look lived in.

### Ring 3 — Chronicle Escapes the Screen (the body and the house)
- **The Beads on the wrist.** Each bead a haptic pulse on a watch — the
  full sequence prayed with eyes closed, phone in pocket. Closer to what
  beads were than any screen can be.
- **The Office read aloud** while you make coffee. The app as liturgist,
  not chatbot.
- **Your voice on the stones.** A stone is not just text: *"press to hear
  Grandpa pray this prayer."*
- **The annual volume.** Each January, last year's chapter arrives
  printed. Closing a volume is itself a ceremony.
- **The home altar.** An e-ink frame in the kitchen — the verse of the
  hour, today's feast, a stone from this week. No notifications, nothing
  to check. The family Bible used to live open on a stand in the parlor.
  Chronicle as furniture, not app.

### Ring 4 — The Braid (the household and the generation)
- A marriage is two threads running parallel, with shared stones set on
  both at once.
- A household is a braid. Family worship is the moment the family's
  threads visibly pass through the same point.
- A child's thread **begins on their parents' thread** — their baptism is
  a stone on three lines simultaneously.
- **The thread that begins before birth.** Letters, prayers, and blessings
  accumulate sealed on a child's thread for years. At thirteen or
  eighteen, they receive it: a childhood's worth of their parents'
  prayers, dated, in your voice.
- **The child's first stone.** Entries are not only text. A four-year-old's
  prayer transcribed verbatim; a six-year-old's crayon ark, photographed.
  Priceless at forty, when that child sets them beside their own
  children's prayers.
- **The relay.** A family scattered across time zones: as one closes her
  evening Office, another's morning Office opens. The Moravians kept a
  prayer watch unbroken for a hundred years. The braid shows the family's
  prayer passing like a flame around the planet.

### Ring 5 — Beyond a Lifetime (the century)
- **Software designed to outlive its user.** The thread ends, and
  Chronicle is honest about that. A memorial mode, designed with dignity:
  the line completes, the Book closes, the thread is passed. A grandchild
  in 2066 opens Chronicle and their line begins where yours ended, every
  stone still there to touch, your voice still on them.
- **The widow's thread.** Grief does not want an archive; it wants *him*.
  On the anniversary, her morning Office quietly includes a prayer he
  recorded, in his own words. She prays through his prayers the way widows
  have prayed through a dead husband's annotated Bible for centuries —
  except his marginalia is forty years deep and searchable by the passage
  she is sitting in.
- **The communion of saints.** The thread format predates this product by
  millennia — Augustine's Confessions, David's Psalms, Brainerd's journal,
  Spurgeon through his depressions, Brother Lawrence at the dishes. A
  library of the saints rendered in Chronicle's own visual language. In a
  dry season, walk over to Spurgeon's thread and see where his darkness
  sat, how long it ran, where it broke. You are not the first to walk
  this, and the interface says so.
- **The protocol endgame.** Chronicle's final form is not an app but a
  *format*: an open, published standard for the spiritual record, the way
  email and RSS are standards. The company can die; the app can die; the
  format must not. If great-grandchildren open a thread in 2089 in
  software not yet written, Chronicle succeeded. The product's crowning
  achievement is making itself replaceable.

### Where the map ends
The next ring out from the communion of saints is eternity, and that one
is not ours to build. Chronicle accompanies a person to the very edge of
it, and its last job is to hand off well, having kept every stone. **The
final feature is humility.**

## The Covenant

These are commitments, not features. They are what the identity costs.

1. **The anti-engagement covenant.** Every other app maximizes
   time-in-app. Chronicle succeeds when it *sends you away* — into prayer,
   into the family, into the week. The Office ends by closing itself.
   Chapel mode has nothing to tap. We measure depth of return, not daily
   active use. A formation tool that optimizes for engagement will
   eventually deform the person using it.

2. **The more sacred the moment, the less technology in the room.** AI
   recedes as sacredness increases: full Study Council in the study, a
   quiet suggestion in the journal, *nothing* in the chapel. The
   ceremonies contain no AI at all. Technology as liturgist, never as
   oracle.

3. **Lament is a first-class citizen.** The Psalms are roughly forty
   percent lament and no product has a lament mode. Chronicle gives the
   angry prayer a room, scaffolded by the Psalms of lament. The Question
   Lab holds open questions with the same dignity as answered prayers —
   open for decades if need be. When one resolves after eleven years, that
   is a ceremony, and it is a stone.

4. **The confessional seal.** Chronicle holds the most sensitive dataset a
   person can produce. Local-first, yours forever, never advertising,
   never training data, and a sealed-entry tier that even analytics cannot
   see. For this product, encryption is a theological position.

5. **The Ebenezer was public.** Joshua 4 was national memory, not private
   — the stones were set "so that all the peoples of the earth might
   know." So: an opt-in pipeline from private practice to communal
   testimony. A congregation's shared Answered Light — hundreds of
   anonymized answered prayers gathered into a single wall of light.

6. **The Record and the Book are not the same document.** The Record is
   everything — honest, angry, unedited, under the seal. The Book is what
   passes on. Late in life comes **the winnowing**: the ceremony of
   walking your whole thread and deciding what becomes testament. A diary
   is what happened. A testament is what it meant. Chronicle holds both
   and never confuses them.

7. **Heirloom economics.** You cannot rent an heirloom. Subscriptions die
   with companies; the family Bible outlived every institution that
   touched it. Own it like a Bible: open formats, self-hostable,
   exportable to paper. The business model is part of the theology, or the
   theology is a lie.

8. **Software that ages with its user.** The app at 75 is not the app at
   35 — the type grows as eyes age, and a forty-year thread behaves
   differently than a forty-day one: more remembrance, less capture; the
   archaeology giving way to the winnowing. Chronicle knows its own length
   and matures alongside its keeper.

## The Edges

Real lives are messier than the ideal user. The places software usually
abandons people are exactly where Chronicle must be strongest.

- **The Archaeology.** Most people arrive at 45 with three decades of walk
  behind them, unrecorded. The first experience is not "write today's
  entry" — it is a gentle, guided excavation that sets the stones never
  recorded: the conversion, the baptism, the prayer answered in 1998, the
  season of almost walking away.
- **Oral history mode.** Sit with your grandmother and let Chronicle
  interview *her* — her stones, her voice, recorded — before that
  generation's testimony is gone. This is a feature with a funeral: every
  month it does not exist, stones are lost forever.
- **The mentor's thread.** Paul and Timothy. An older believer selectively
  opens their thread to someone they disciple — specifically the stones
  from the age the apprentice is now. Not advice; evidence.
- **The prodigal design.** Covered in the Design Language, restated here
  because it is a covenant with real families: absence is never punished,
  the braid keeps a place, and a return after nineteen years resumes
  without one pixel of shame.

---

## The No List

Chronicle will not build: social feeds · streaks · leaderboards ·
comparison mechanics of any kind · engagement notifications ·
advertising · data sale · AI inside ceremonies · public follower counts.
Comparison is the thief of formation. The absence of these is a feature
and is to be defended like one.
