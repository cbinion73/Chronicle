Old Family Bible. This is an heirloom, not a museum piece — a real book with a cracked-in leather cover that has sat on a shelf for decades, opened often enough to soften at the spine. Where the app's "Manuscript" register venerates scripture with illumination and gold leaf, this register domesticates it: oxblood leather, brass corner-wear, foxed page edges, and a plain gilt stripe are enough to say this book has been read, not this book is sacred and untouchable. The reading experience should feel like Sunday afternoon at grandmother's house, not a scriptorium.

Calling Clarified

June 15, 2020

It was somewhere between the second cup of coffee and the dog needing to go out again that the thought finally sat still long enough to look at. Not a bolt of lightning — more like a fog lifting off a field I'd been standing in the whole time. I have spent three years asking God to tell me what to do next. This morning I understood the question was wrong. He hasn't been quiet about direction. I've been unwilling to admit the direction was already underneath my feet.

What I do with people — the listening, the follow-up, the remembering what they told me six months ago — isn't a hobby I do around the real work. It is the work. I wrote that sentence down twice, like I didn't trust myself the first time.
